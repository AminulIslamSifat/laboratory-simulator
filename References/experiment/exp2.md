Name of the Experiment: Polarity Test of a Single-Phase Transformer
Objective:
The experimental determination of the polarity of a single-phase transformer i.e. determination
of whether the connections of the windings of the transformer have been made in additive or
subtractive polarity fashion and its significance in the operation of transformer.
In particular, the purpose of this experiment is to:
1. Gain knowledge about the concept of polarity in transformer winding and how the relative
instantaneous directions of induced EMFs in primary and secondary winding give rise to additive
or subtractive polarity.
2. Conduct the test of polarity by making certain connections of transformer windings in test
circuit and taking the readings of voltmeter across the connected terminals.
3. Differentiate between the additive polarity (where voltmeter reading is the addition of the
two voltages) and subtractive polarity (where voltmeter reading is the subtraction of the two
voltages).
4. Identify the marking of transformer terminals (H1, H2, X1, X2) on the basis of the test
performed.
5. Realize the significance of determination of polarity in practical applications like parallel
operation of transformers, connection in three-phase banks, and installation of instrument
transformers – where wrong polarity leads to large short-circuits current.
Apparatus:
● Single-phase transformer
● AC voltage source
● AC voltmeters
● Connecting wires and a jumper wire
Theory
It is very important to identify the polarity of the winding terminals of the transformer before
installing it in the circuit, for instance, parallel connection, in case of a single phase transformer.
In a polarity test, the direction of the induced EMF on the secondary winding relative to the
primary winding is going to be determined.The procedure of conducting the test is to connect one terminal of the primary winding to one
terminal of the secondary winding. A reduced AC voltage denoted by Va is applied on the
primary winding. The induced voltage on the secondary winding is Vb. Measure the voltage at
the other two terminals denoted by Vc.

AdditiveDiscussion:
In Trial 1, it is noted that the value of resultant voltage Vc (92 V) was smaller than the value of
Va (100 V) and satisfied the equation Vc = Va – Vb. This gives the condition for the subtractive
polarity.
In Trials 2, 3, and 4, the value of Vc was higher than Va and satisfied the equation Vc = Va +
Vb, giving the condition for additive polarity.
The small deviations noted in the values of theory and experiment may be due to the following
reasons:
a) Error in measuring devices (voltmeters).
b) Change in the supply/input voltage.
c) Loose contacts at the jumper terminal ends.
Conclusion:
Furthermore, the polarity test has successfully been performed for the single phase transformer
provided. Comparing the input voltage Va and the output voltage Vc, it is found that the polarity
of the terminals of the transformer is always additive/subtractive. It can be concluded from this
experiment that the polarity should always be checked before paralleling of the transformers.
